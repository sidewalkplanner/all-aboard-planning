### Answer Key: Set 2

21. **(b)** The rational planning process runs: goals/vision → inventory and analysis → alternatives → selection → implementation → monitoring. Skipping the inventory means the plan rests on no data.
22. **(b)** TDR moves development rights from a sending area (farmland) to a receiving area (growth zone) via private sale. PDR uses public money; UGBs and ag zoning don't compensate landowners.
23. **(c)** The CIP is the multi-year (typically 5–6 year) schedule of capital projects and funding sources. The operating budget covers annual recurring costs.
24. **(b)** PUDs trade flexibility (I), mixed uses (II), and clustering for open space (IV) for negotiated approval. Strict conformance to the base district's use list (III) is what a PUD relaxes.
25. **(b)** TIF freezes the base assessed value; the "increment" above it repays bonds for district improvements. (a) is a special assessment district; (c) is an impact fee.
26. **(b)** Rational nexus/rough proportionality (Nollan/Dolan lineage): fees must be tied to the development's demand and earmarked for facilities serving it, not general revenue.
27. **(b)** An overlay adds requirements on top of the base zoning without changing it. A floating zone is unmapped until applied; spot zoning is generally illegal.
28. **(c)** LOS runs A (free flow) to F (breakdown). LOS E is at capacity.
29. **(b)** ITE's *Trip Generation Manual*.
30. **(b)** 30%; "severely cost-burdened" is more than 50%.
31. **(b)** EA first; if no significant impact → FONSI; if significant → EIS → ROD. A categorical exclusion applies only to actions already known to lack significant effects.
32. **(b)** Section 106 requires federal agencies to consider effects on historic properties and consult the SHPO. Section 4(f) may also apply to DOT projects, so "only" makes (a) wrong.
33. **(c)** Bottom rungs (manipulation, therapy) = nonparticipation; middle (informing, consultation, placation) = tokenism; top (partnership, delegated power, citizen control) = citizen power.
34. **(b)** Communicate to the audience's level; be transparent about assumptions and uncertainty. Hiding the range (c) is misleading; dumping tables (a) is ineffective.
35. **(c)** Zero-based budgeting. Line-item tracks inputs; performance ties to outputs; PPBS ties to program objectives.
36. **(b)** Leadership questions favor coaching, clear expectations, and support before escalation. Reassigning or silently extending are both avoidance.
37. **(b)** Moore struck down limits on related family members living together; Belle Terre allows limits on unrelated occupants.
38. **(b)** Reed: distinctions based on the sign's message are content-based and must survive strict scrutiny. Regulate by size, location, and lighting instead.
39. **(b)** McHarg's transparent-overlay suitability mapping. Lynch = image of the city; Geddes = "survey before plan."
40. **(c)** 50,000 × 1.02^10 ≈ 50,000 × 1.219 ≈ 60,950. Linear growth (2% of 50,000 × 10) would give 60,000 — a common trap.

### Answer Key: Set 3

41. **(c)** The Rules of Conduct bar accepting gifts or favors that could compromise, or appear to compromise, professional judgment while a matter is pending. Disclosure does not cure a gift that should be declined.
42. **(b)** She cannot serve where her firm has a competing financial interest; the correct move is disclosure and declining, not partial participation.
43. **(c)** Planners must not knowingly participate in deception or misrepresentation and must ensure accurate information. Address it internally first and correct the record; go to the Ethics Officer if the firm refuses to act. Altering a colleague's work secretly (d) is itself misconduct.
44. **(a)** Kelo affirmed Berman's broad "public purpose" reading; many states then restricted eminent domain by statute.
45. **(b)** Mount Laurel I established the fair-share obligation; Mount Laurel II added the builder's remedy and led to the NJ Fair Housing Act.
46. **(b)** Riis's photojournalism drove tenement reform; the 1901 "New Law" required light, air, and sanitation.
47. **(b)** NYC 1916, prompted by the Equitable Building and garment-district encroachment on Fifth Avenue. LA had earlier use districts (1908) but not a comprehensive ordinance.
48. **(b)** Simon's bounded rationality: "administrative man" satisfices; "economic man" maximizes.
49. **(b)** Mixed scanning sits between the rational model and incrementalism.
50. **(b)** Shift-share = national + industry mix + local share. Location quotient is a separate economic base technique.
51. **(b)** Median resists outliers; the mean is pulled upward by extreme values.
52. **(c)** Stratified sampling divides the population into subgroups and samples each proportionally.
53. **(b)** LIHTC is an IRS program allocated by state HFAs to developers, who sell credits to investors for equity.
54. **(b)** BIDs are self-assessment districts. TIF captures tax increment; enterprise zones offer tax incentives in distressed areas.
55. **(c)** Lynch's five: paths, edges, districts, nodes, landmarks.
56. **(b)** 1% annual chance; the "500-year" floodplain is 0.2%.
57. **(b)** DMA 2000 ties FEMA mitigation funding to an approved local hazard mitigation plan, updated every five years.
58. **(b)** The facilitator manages process, not outcome; neutrality in facilitation is distinct from advocacy in analysis.
59. **(b)** CPM/PERT identifies the critical path. Gantt charts show schedules but not task dependencies.
60. **(b)** Spot zoning: a small parcel, singled out for the owner's benefit, inconsistent with the plan and surrounding uses. Contract zoning involves a bilateral agreement with the owner.
