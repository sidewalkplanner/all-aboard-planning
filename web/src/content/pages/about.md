## Why we built the course

Most planners prepare for the AICP exam while working full time. The material is broad, running from nineteenth-century reform movements to impact-fee case law to running a public meeting, and the available resources are often scattered, out of date, or written as lists of facts to memorize.

We built the course we wanted when we studied:

- **Organized around the exam.** Lessons follow the nine domains of APA's exam content outline, and practice is weighted the way the real exam is.
- **Written to be understood.** Every lesson explains why a rule or idea exists, then shows it in real planning situations, because the exam tests judgment as much as recall.
- **Connected.** Every lesson links to the questions that test it, and every score report links back to the lessons.
- **Honest about what practice can tell you.** The real exam is scaled and APA doesn't publish a passing percentage, so we never promise that a practice score predicts a result.

## How we write the content

- **Original work.** Every lesson and question is written from scratch. We use APA's published content outline as a blueprint, and don't copy APA materials, textbooks, other prep courses, or anything recalled from a live exam.
- **Accuracy first.** We work from primary sources for cases, statutes, and dates, and we update the lessons when the law or APA's rules change.
- **Found an error?** Please [tell us](/contact). Reports from planners using the material are the best quality check we have.

## Independence

All Aboard Planning is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by the American Planning Association (APA) or the American Institute of Certified Planners (AICP). We use those names only to describe the exam the course prepares you for.
