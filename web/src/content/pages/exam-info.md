## What the exam is

The AICP Certification Exam is the test planners pass to earn the AICP credential from the American Institute of Certified Planners, APA's professional institute. It measures whether you can apply planning knowledge and professional judgment across the full range of practice, not just recall facts.

The questions are built from APA's exam content outline, which groups the material into nine domains:

| # | Domain | Approximate share of the exam |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Research and Assessment Methods | 11% |
| 2 | Fundamental Planning Knowledge | 15% |
| 3 | Communication and Interaction | 13% |
| 4 | Plan and Policy Development | 15% |
| 5 | Plan Implementation | 12% |
| 6 | Administration and Management | 6% |
| 7 | Leadership | 6% |
| 8 | Areas of Practice | 12% |
| 9 | AICP Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct | 10% |

## Format

- **Question type.** Multiple choice, with four answer options and one best answer. Expect standalone questions, groups of questions that share a scenario, questions built around a table or chart, and a few that ask you to calculate something (such as density, floor area ratio, or a location quotient).
- **Length.** 170 questions in 3.5 hours (210 minutes). That works out to roughly 74 seconds per question. <!-- VERIFY: confirm the current question count and time limit with APA. -->
- **Unscored questions.** Some questions are pretest items that don't count toward your score, and you won't know which ones they are. Answer every question as if it counts. <!-- VERIFY: confirm that APA still includes unscored pretest items, and how many. -->
- **Delivery.** The exam is taken on a computer, either at a testing center or with remote online proctoring, through APA's testing vendor. <!-- VERIFY: confirm the current vendor (historically Prometric) and whether remote proctoring is still offered. -->
- **No penalty for guessing.** Your score is based on the questions you answer correctly, so never leave a question blank. <!-- VERIFY: confirm APA's current scoring policy on unanswered questions. -->

## Scoring

APA reports a **scaled score** rather than a raw percentage, so the number of questions you need to answer correctly can differ slightly from one exam form to another. Historically, scores have been reported on a scale of 25 to 75, with 55 as the passing score. <!-- VERIFY: confirm the current score scale and passing score with APA. -->

Because the exam is scaled and APA doesn't publish a passing percentage, **no practice score (ours or anyone else's) can tell you whether you will pass.** Use practice scores to decide what to study, and use full-length timed exams to build pace.

## Eligibility

To take the exam, you apply to APA and show a combination of education and professional planning experience. In general, the more planning-specific your degree, the less experience you need. A degree from a program accredited by the Planning Accreditation Board (PAB) has historically shortened the experience requirement.

The combinations APA has used in the past look like this:

| Education | Professional planning experience |
|---|---|
| Graduate degree from a PAB-accredited planning program | 2 years |
| Bachelor's degree from a PAB-accredited planning program | 3 years |
| Graduate degree in another field | 3 years |
| Bachelor's degree in another field | 4 years |
| No college degree | 8 years |

"Professional planning experience" has a specific meaning: work that applies a planning process with appropriate professional responsibility and that influences public decision-making. APA decides what qualifies, so describe your experience carefully in your application. <!-- VERIFY: confirm APA's current definition of qualifying professional planning experience. -->

## How to register

The process usually runs in this order. APA's site has the current steps, deadlines, and fees.

1. **Check your eligibility** against APA's current requirements.
2. **Apply to APA** during the application period for the testing window you want, and document your education and experience. <!-- VERIFY: confirm whether the application still requires written responses about experience (historically called "criteria" essays). -->
3. **Wait for approval.** APA reviews your application before you can schedule.
4. **Pay the exam fee** and **schedule your appointment** with the testing vendor, either at a test center or online.
5. **Request testing accommodations** at the time APA specifies, if you need them. APA provides accommodations for candidates with disabilities. <!-- VERIFY: confirm APA's accommodation request process and deadline. -->
6. **Take the exam** during your testing window. The exam is offered in set testing windows during the year, historically in May and November. <!-- VERIFY: confirm current testing window months. -->

> **Plan backward from the testing window.** Application deadlines come well before the exam. Most of our [study plans](/aicp/study-plan) run 8 or 12 weeks, so start studying once your application is in rather than waiting for approval.

## On exam day

For pacing, question formats, and test-taking tactics, see the [exam strategy guide](/aicp/review/exam-strategy). The basics:


- Bring the identification the testing vendor requires, and arrive (or log in) early.
- Read each question's last sentence first so you know what it's asking, then read the scenario.
- Flag questions you're unsure about, keep moving, and come back to them. Our practice exams let you flag and review in the same way.
- Watch for **EXCEPT**, **NOT**, and **LEAST** in the question stem.
- Check what's permitted (such as an on-screen calculator) in APA's candidate materials. <!-- VERIFY: confirm calculator and scratch-paper policies with APA's current candidate guide. -->

## After you pass

Once you pass, you can use the AICP designation. To keep it, you earn **Certification Maintenance (CM)** credits: historically 32 credits every two years, including a set number in ethics, planning law, equity, and sustainability and resilience.
