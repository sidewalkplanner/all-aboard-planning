## Learning objectives

- Delegate effectively and resolve conflict between staff members.
- Distinguish coaching, mentoring, and sponsorship, and describe what makes mentoring work.
- Plan for succession and measure whether leadership development is working.
- Explain how leaders build accountability and inclusive practice into institutions.
- Describe professional development expectations for certified planners, including Certification Maintenance.

## Key concepts

### Delegation

Delegation develops staff and frees leaders to lead, but only if it's real. Effective delegation gives the person:

- clear **expectations**: what outcome, by when, to what standard;
- the **authority** to make the decisions the task requires; and
- **accountability**: agreed check-ins and ownership of the result.

Match the task to the person's readiness, and increase the challenge over time. Delegating responsibility without authority sets people up to fail.

### Resolving staff conflict

When two staff members are in an escalating dispute, the leader's best approach is usually to **meet with each separately** to understand their perspectives, then **convene a facilitated conversation** focused on their underlying **interests** and the work, not on personalities or blame. Agree on specific next steps and follow up. Ignoring conflict lets it spread; imposing a solution without listening rarely lasts.

### Coaching, mentoring, and sponsorship

- **Coaching** focuses on specific skills or performance, usually over a defined period: presenting to a commission, writing staff reports, running a meeting.
- **Mentoring** is a longer-term relationship centered on professional and career growth: judgment, values, navigating the organization, and long-term goals.
- **Sponsorship** means using your influence to open doors, such as recommending a junior planner for a visible assignment or a promotion.

Mentoring an early-career planner is most effective when the mentor provides **progressively challenging assignments with candid feedback**, plus **guidance and opportunity**: exposure to decision-makers, networks, and stretch projects. Mentoring serves the individual, and it also **develops the profession's future capacity**.

### Succession planning

Many planning agencies face waves of retirements. A director preparing for turnover should focus first on **developing internal candidates**:

- identify critical roles and the knowledge they hold;
- cross-train and rotate assignments;
- document key processes and institutional knowledge;
- give emerging leaders real responsibility (acting roles, project leadership); and
- support training and credentials.

The effectiveness of leadership development is best measured by **internal promotion rates, retention, and succession readiness**, not by the number of trainings held.

### Institutional structures and accountability

Leaders build accountability into how an organization works, not just into individual behavior:

- clear roles, decision rules, and **delegated authority**;
- transparent reporting on performance and on how public money is spent;
- upholding the **AICP Code of Ethics** and organizational ethics policies;
- being honest about mistakes and correcting them; and
- structures that let staff raise concerns without retaliation.

A professional planning leader demonstrates accountability by upholding the Code, being transparent about decisions, and taking responsibility for results.

### Equity, diversity, and inclusion in practice

Planning organizations that reflect and understand their communities make better plans. Leaders advance equity inside the organization through inclusive recruitment and hiring, fair pay and promotion practices, mentoring and sponsorship for people from groups underrepresented in the profession, and a workplace culture where different perspectives are heard. The AICP Code's aspirational principles ask planners to seek social justice and expand choice for all, and those commitments apply inside the office as well as in the community.

### Professional development expectations

The profession expects planners to keep learning throughout their careers:

- The AICP Code's Rules of Conduct require members to provide only services they're **competent** to perform, or to work under the supervision of someone who is (see [Conflicts of interest and the Rules of Conduct](/aicp/lessons/conflicts-of-interest-and-rules-of-conduct)).
- **Certification Maintenance (CM)**: to keep the AICP credential, members earn CM credits over each reporting period. - AICP also offers **advanced specialty certifications** for experienced planners in fields such as environmental planning, transportation planning, and urban design. <!-- VERIFY: confirm which advanced specialty certifications AICP currently offers. -->
- APA chapters and divisions, conferences, and volunteer roles on boards and commissions are common routes to growth and to giving back.

## Key terms

- **Delegation**: Assigning responsibility and authority for a task, with accountability.
- **Coaching**: Short-term development of specific skills or performance.
- **Mentoring**: A longer-term relationship supporting professional and career growth.
- **Sponsorship**: Using one's influence to create opportunities for someone else.
- **Stretch assignment**: A task just beyond a person's current experience, used for growth.
- **Succession planning**: Preparing internal candidates to fill key roles.
- **Knowledge transfer**: Capturing and sharing institutional knowledge before people leave.
- **Certification Maintenance (CM)**: The continuing education AICP members must complete to keep certification.
- **Accountability**: Answering for decisions and results, openly.

## Real-world examples

**A mentoring program.** A county planning department pairs each new planner with a senior mentor outside their direct chain of command. Mentors commit to monthly conversations and to bringing their mentee to at least one commission meeting and one interagency negotiation a quarter. Supervisors assign each new planner one "stretch" project a year. Within three years, two mentees have moved into project manager roles.

**Preparing for retirements.** With five of twelve senior staff eligible to retire within four years, a planning director creates an acting-supervisor rotation, has departing staff record walkthroughs of key processes, and funds AICP exam preparation for junior staff. The department tracks internal promotions and retention as its measures of success.

**Mediating a team conflict.** Two senior planners clash over whether a housing study should emphasize production or tenant protections. The director meets with each, learns both share the goal of reducing displacement, and runs a facilitated session where they agree to structure the study around both production and preservation.

## Exam tips

- Effective delegation gives **expectations, authority, and accountability**.
- Good mentoring means **progressively challenging assignments with candid feedback**.
- Staff conflict: meet with **each** person, then facilitate a conversation about **interests**.
- Measure leadership development by **promotion, retention, and succession readiness**.
- Keeping AICP certification requires **Certification Maintenance** credits, including ethics and law.

## Summary

Delegate with clear expectations, real authority, and accountability. Handle staff conflict by listening to each person, then facilitating a conversation about interests. Coaching builds specific skills, mentoring supports long-term growth through progressively challenging work and candid feedback, and sponsorship opens doors. Prepare for turnover by developing internal candidates, and measure success by promotion, retention, and readiness. Build accountability and inclusion into institutions, not just individuals. Certified planners keep learning through Certification Maintenance and must work within their competence.
