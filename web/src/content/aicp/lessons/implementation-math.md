## Learning objectives

- Convert between square feet, acres, and square miles without hesitation.
- Calculate floor area ratio, building height from FAR and coverage, and lot coverage.
- Calculate gross and net density, and units allowed under a minimum-lot-area rule or density bonus.
- Calculate required parking and the land area it consumes, and peak-hour trip generation.
- Calculate housing cost burden, affordable monthly housing cost, and income as a percentage of area median income.

## Key concepts

The AICP exam includes a handful of calculation questions. None needs advanced math, but they reward planners who set up the problem carefully. Each section below gives the formula, then a worked example. Try each example yourself before reading the answer.

### Units you must know

- **1 acre = 43,560 square feet**
- **1 square mile = 640 acres**
- A percentage is a fraction of 100: 30% = 0.30.

### Floor area ratio (FAR)

> **FAR = total building floor area ÷ lot area**

FAR controls bulk without dictating shape: a FAR of 2.0 allows a two-story building covering the whole lot, a four-story building covering half of it, or an eight-story tower covering a quarter.

> **Stories ≈ FAR ÷ lot coverage** (when every floor has the same footprint)

**Worked example.** A two-acre downtown site is approved at a FAR of 2.0, and the building footprint covers 50% of the site. How many stories?

- Lot area: 2 × 43,560 = 87,120 sq ft
- Maximum floor area: 87,120 × 2.0 = 174,240 sq ft
- Footprint: 87,120 × 0.50 = 43,560 sq ft
- Stories: 174,240 ÷ 43,560 = **4** (or simply 2.0 ÷ 0.50 = 4)

### Lot coverage

> **Lot coverage = building footprint (or impervious area) ÷ lot area**

**Worked example.** A 10,000 sq ft lot has a 2,500 sq ft house and a 500 sq ft garage. Building coverage = 3,000 ÷ 10,000 = **30%**.

### Gross and net density

- **Gross density** = units ÷ *total* site area.
- **Net density** = units ÷ area remaining *after* subtracting streets, required open space, and other non-residential land.

Net density is always higher than gross density for the same project. Always check which one an ordinance uses.

**Worked example.** A 40-acre parcel is zoned for 6 dwelling units per **net** acre, and 25% of the site must go to streets and required open space. How many units?

- Net area: 40 × (1 − 0.25) = 30 acres
- Units: 30 × 6 = **180**
- Gross density: 180 ÷ 40 = 4.5 units per gross acre

### Minimum lot area per unit

Some codes set density as a minimum amount of land per dwelling unit.

**Worked example.** A 12,000 sq ft lot is in a district requiring 1,500 sq ft of lot area per unit. Units allowed: 12,000 ÷ 1,500 = **8**. (Round *down* when a result isn't a whole number. You can't build part of a unit, and rounding up would exceed the limit, unless the code says otherwise.)

### Density bonuses

**Worked example.** Base zoning allows 100 units, and a 20% bonus is available for including affordable housing. Total allowed: 100 × 1.20 = **120** units.

### Parking

> **Required spaces = floor area ÷ 1,000 × spaces per 1,000 sq ft** (or units × spaces per unit)

**Worked example.** A 60,000 sq ft retail center must provide 4 spaces per 1,000 sq ft of gross floor area. Spaces: 60,000 ÷ 1,000 × 4 = **240**.

**How much land does that take?** If each space plus its share of drive aisles averages 350 sq ft:

- 240 × 350 = 84,000 sq ft
- 84,000 ÷ 43,560 ≈ **1.93 acres**, just under two acres, which is larger than the building's own footprint on a one-story center.

This is why parking minimums matter so much for land use. Many cities have reduced or eliminated them, especially near transit.

### Trip generation

> **Trips = units (or floor area) × trip rate**

Trip rates usually come from published trip generation data or local studies.

**Worked example.** A 200-unit apartment building generates 0.5 PM peak-hour trips per unit. PM peak-hour trips: 200 × 0.5 = **100**.

### Housing affordability

The standard planning benchmark: a household is **cost-burdened** when it spends **more than 30%** of gross income on housing costs, and **severely cost-burdened** above **50%**. See [Housing and community development](/aicp/lessons/housing-and-community-development).

> **Cost burden = annual housing cost ÷ annual gross income**
> **Maximum affordable monthly cost = annual income × 0.30 ÷ 12**
> **Income as % of AMI = household income ÷ area median income × 100**

**Worked example.** A household earns $42,000 a year and pays $1,300 a month in rent and utilities. The area median income for a household of its size is $84,000.

- Annual housing cost: 1,300 × 12 = $15,600
- Cost burden: 15,600 ÷ 42,000 ≈ **37%**, so the household is cost-burdened
- Affordable at 30%: 42,000 × 0.30 ÷ 12 = **$1,050 a month**
- Income as a share of AMI: 42,000 ÷ 84,000 = **50% of AMI**

### Percent change and growth rates

> **Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100**

**Worked example.** Employment rose from 8,000 to 9,200 jobs. Percent change: (9,200 − 8,000) ÷ 8,000 = 0.15 = **15%**.

For projections, location quotients, and shift-share, see [Population projections and economic analysis](/aicp/lessons/demographic-and-economic-analysis). For present value and benefit-cost ratios, see [Statistics and quantitative evaluation](/aicp/lessons/statistics-for-planners).

## Key terms

- **Acre**: 43,560 square feet.
- **Floor area ratio (FAR)**: Total floor area divided by lot area.
- **Lot coverage**: Footprint (or impervious area) divided by lot area.
- **Gross density**: Units per total site area.
- **Net density**: Units per site area after subtracting streets, open space, and other non-residential land.
- **Minimum lot area per unit**: A density rule expressed as land required for each dwelling unit.
- **Parking ratio**: Required parking spaces per unit of floor area or per dwelling unit.
- **Trip generation rate**: Average trips produced per unit of a land use in a given period.
- **Cost-burdened household**: A household paying more than 30% of gross income for housing.
- **Area median income (AMI)**: The median household income for an area, used to set affordability limits.

## Exam tips

- **Write the formula first**, then plug in numbers. Most errors come from mixing up which area (gross or net, lot or footprint) goes where.
- **Convert units before dividing.** If the lot is in acres and the floor area in square feet, convert first.
- **Check reasonableness.** A FAR of 2.0 on a site with 50% coverage can't produce 12 stories.
- **Round only at the end**, and round unit counts down unless told otherwise.

## Real-world examples

**Testing a zoning proposal.** A council member proposes raising the downtown FAR from 3.0 to 6.0. Staff model what that means on a typical 20,000 sq ft lot: 120,000 sq ft of floor area, or about 12 stories at 50% coverage. The math makes the choice concrete for the public.

**Right-sizing parking.** A grocery store proposal must provide 5 spaces per 1,000 sq ft on a 40,000 sq ft store: 200 spaces. At roughly 350 sq ft each, that's about 1.6 acres of asphalt. Data from comparable stores shows peak demand closer to 3 spaces per 1,000 sq ft. The city amends the requirement and the store adds a small plaza in the space saved.

## Summary

Know that an acre is 43,560 square feet and a square mile is 640 acres. FAR is floor area over lot area, and stories are roughly FAR divided by coverage. Net density excludes streets and open space; gross density doesn't. Parking and trip generation multiply size by a rate, and parking often consumes more land than the building. Cost burden is housing cost over income (above 30% is burdened, above 50% severe), the affordable monthly cost is 30% of annual income divided by 12, and income divided by AMI gives the income category. Write the formula, convert units, and check that the answer makes sense.
